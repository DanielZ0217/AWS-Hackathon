# ECG rhythm findings and monitoring limits

Educational, source-linked summary for a synthetic-data prototype. It does not interpret an actual waveform.

An ECG records the heart's electrical signals. A clinician can use it to assess heart rate, rhythm, and the timing of activity through the heart. An unusual tracing may be a clue, but a single ECG cannot diagnose a heart condition on its own; symptoms, history, and other tests are needed. Because a standard ECG samples a short period, it may miss a problem that comes and goes. A clinician may use longer-term monitoring when that question remains. [Source: MedlinePlus electrocardiogram](https://medlineplus.gov/lab-tests/electrocardiogram/).

If this prototype receives only a written ECG report, it must describe the report as supplied text, not claim to have inspected the signal, leads, intervals, or waveform. Preserve whether a reported finding was confirmed, uncertain, or nonspecific.
