# What different heart images can show

Educational, source-linked summary for a synthetic-data prototype. No image was analyzed to produce this text.

A chest X-ray depicts the chest, including the heart and lungs, and may reveal signs that lead a clinician to investigate heart or lung disease. Cardiac CT uses X-rays to create detailed views of the heart and blood vessels. Cardiac MRI uses magnets and radio waves to make detailed heart images. Echocardiography uses sound waves to show heart structures and movement, including how chambers and valves work. These methods answer different questions and are not interchangeable. [Source: MedlinePlus heart health tests](https://medlineplus.gov/hearthealthtests.html).

An image finding can have more than one explanation. The radiology persona must distinguish an actual image assessment from a supplied report and must retain the modality, body region, view or sequence, acquisition date, and stated uncertainty. The Doctor persona combines imaging with history, examination, and other tests rather than treating imaging as a standalone diagnosis. [Source: NHLBI heart-failure diagnosis](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/heart-failure/diagnosis).
